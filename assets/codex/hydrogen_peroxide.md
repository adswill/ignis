# Hydrogen peroxide (HTP)
*Water with an extra oxygen atom, aching to let it go.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Formula | H₂O₂ |
| Density | ≈1390 kg/m³ (90 %) |
| Decomposition | 2 H₂O₂ → 2 H₂O + O₂, -98 kJ/mol |
| 90 % HTP decomposition temperature | ≈740 °C (≈1020 K), adiabatic |
| Pharmacy strength | 3 % |

## In the real world
High-test peroxide (85-98 %) decomposes on catalysts like silver or permanganate into superheated steam and oxygen, with no flame needed. That made it a monopropellant for the Bell rocket belt, torpedoes and the Me 163's fuel system, and an oxidiser for Britain's Black Arrow rocket. A leaking peroxide torpedo is the likely cause of the Kursk submarine disaster (2000).

**Uses:** Bleaching paper and hair, disinfectant, rocket monopropellant and oxidiser, elephant-toothpaste demos.

**Hazards:** Concentrated peroxide sets organic material on fire and causes severe burns; contamination can cause runaway decomposition.

## In Ignis
- Decomposes on [[manganese_dioxide]], [[potassium_permanganate]], [[rust]] or iron, or when hot (above **≈420 K** it runs away), into oxygen + steam at up to **≈1020 K**.

## Recipes
- Peroxide + [[manganese_dioxide]] → oxygen + steam ([[peroxide_decomposition]]).

## Try this
Make a peroxide tank with an MnO₂ layer at the bottom and a narrow outlet: a monopropellant rocket. Put a flame at the exit.
