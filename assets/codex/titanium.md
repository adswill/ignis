# Titanium
*Steel's strength at 57 % of the weight, with a grudge against heat.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Density | 4430 kg/m³ (Ti-6Al-4V) |
| Melting point | 1668 °C pure (1941 K); Ti-6Al-4V ≈1604-1660 °C |
| Thermal conductivity | ≈6.7 W/(m·K) (Ti-6Al-4V), 7× worse than steel |
| Yield strength | ≈880 MPa (Ti-6Al-4V) |
| Max service temperature | ≈400-550 °C |

## In the real world
Titanium alloys have the best strength-to-weight ratio of the common structural metals and shrug off corrosion. Their poor heat conduction means heat stays where it lands, and above ~550 °C titanium begins to absorb oxygen and turn brittle. A titanium fire (it burns in air once molten or as chips) is extremely hot.

**Uses:** Jet engine fan blades and discs, airframes (the SR-71 was about 85 % titanium), medical implants, high-pressure gas bottles.

**Hazards:** Fine titanium chips and powder are flammable; burning titanium cannot be put out with water or CO₂.

## In Ignis
- The strongest wall: strength **6.5**. Ideal for vaults and high-pressure chambers.
- Softens at **≈600 K**, melts at **≈1680 K**.
- Poor conductor (0.5× steel): a flame touching it makes a very hot local spot, so keep hot gas off titanium or cool it.
