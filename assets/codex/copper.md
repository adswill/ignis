# Copper
*The best practical heat conductor: it carries heat away before it can pile up.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Density | 8960 kg/m³ |
| Melting point | 1084.6 °C (1357.8 K) |
| Boiling point | 2562 °C |
| Thermal conductivity | 401 W/(m·K), ≈8× steel |
| Specific heat | 385 J/(kg·K) |
| Yield strength | ≈70 MPa annealed |

## In the real world
Only silver conducts heat better among common metals. Copper was the first metal humans worked (≈9000 BC) and is still the material of choice when heat has to be moved: liquid-rocket combustion chambers, CPU heat spreaders, cookware bottoms. Pure copper is soft; rocket chambers use copper alloys like NARloy-Z (Cu-Ag-Zr) or GRCop-84 to keep strength when hot.

**Uses:** Wiring, heat exchangers, rocket chamber liners, plumbing, coins.

**Hazards:** Molten copper vapour and copper oxide fumes are toxic (metal fume fever).

## In Ignis
- Strength **1.5**, softens at **≈480 K**, melts at **≈1080 K**.
- Highest conductivity of the walls (3.6× steel): it pulls heat out of a hot spot and spreads it along the whole wall.
- Combine with active cooling: see [[cooled_copper]].

## Try this
Put a torch on one end of a long copper bar and a long steel bar. Switch to the **Heat** view: the copper glows along its whole length while the steel stays hot only at the tip.
