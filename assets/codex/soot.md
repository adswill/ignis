# Soot & smoke
*Tiny carbon particles: they make flames yellow and smoke black.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Primary particle size | ≈10-50 nm, clumped into chains |
| Forms | in fuel-rich zones above ≈1300 K |
| Glows | yellow-orange at ≈1500-2000 K (black-body radiation) |
| Radiant fraction of a sooty pool fire | ≈30-40 % of its heat |

## Formation
When fuel is heated without enough oxygen it breaks down into small fragments (acetylene, radicals) that build up into ring-shaped molecules (polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons), then nanoscale carbon particles. This happens in the fuel-rich interior of diffusion flames.

## Why flames are yellow
A clean premixed flame is blue (light from excited CH and C₂ molecules). Hot soot glows like a tiny black body, giving the familiar yellow-orange. Most soot burns up as it reaches oxygen at the edge of the flame; what escapes is smoke.

## Why it matters
Soot radiation carries most of the heat from large fires to their surroundings, which is how fires spread across rooms. Soot is also a major air pollutant and climate forcer.

## In Ignis
- Hot fuel without enough oxygen cracks into soot; soot burns when it meets oxygen, and radiates heat away, cooling the flame.
- Smoke density and colour are drawn from the soot field. Kerosene, plastics, TNT and rich flames are sooty.

## Try this
Light a [[kerosene]] pool, then blow air into its base with a [[fan]]. The flame gets brighter, smaller and cleaner.
