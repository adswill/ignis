# Buoyancy & plumes
*Hot gas is light, so it rises: the reason flames point up.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Ideal gas density | ρ = p · M / (R · T): at 1500 K air is 1/5 as dense as at 300 K |
| Fire plume velocity | ≈1-10 m/s above ordinary fires |
| Candle flame in orbit | spherical, blue, dim (no buoyancy) |

## Why flames point up
At constant pressure, gas density falls as temperature rises. Hot combustion products are pushed upward by the heavier cold air around them, dragging in fresh air at the base. This **natural convection** feeds every campfire and candle.

## Plumes and chimneys
Above a fire, the rising column entrains surrounding air, widening and cooling as it rises. Chimneys exploit the **stack effect**: a column of hot gas weighs less than an equal column of outside air, so the pressure difference pulls air through the fire.

## Without gravity
On the International Space Station a candle flame becomes a dim blue sphere: no buoyancy means no convection, so oxygen arrives only by slow diffusion.

## In Ignis
- Gravity pulls cold, dense gas down and lets hot gas rise. Methane, lighter than air, rises too; CO₂ from dry ice sinks.
- Turn gravity off (if your build exposes the gravity setting) and watch flames go round.

## Try this
Light a campfire, then turn gravity to zero. Then build a chimney over a fire and compare its burn rate with an open fire.
