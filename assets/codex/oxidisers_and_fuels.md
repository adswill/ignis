# Oxidisers & fuels
*Every reaction that makes fire is one thing taking electrons from another.*

## Fuels
Fuels hold electrons in relatively weak bonds: hydrocarbons, carbohydrates, carbon, metals (aluminium, magnesium), sulfur.

## Oxidisers
Oxidisers grab electrons: oxygen gas, but also solids that hold oxygen like **nitrates** ([[saltpeter]], [[ammonium_nitrate]]), **perchlorates** ([[ammonium_perchlorate]], [[potassium_perchlorate]]), **permanganate**, **peroxides** and **metal oxides** (rust, copper oxide in thermite).

## Why solid oxidisers change everything
In air, a fuel can only burn as fast as oxygen reaches it. Mix a solid oxidiser into the fuel at the grain level and every particle has its oxygen right next to it: burn rates jump by orders of magnitude, and the mixture burns underwater, in vacuum and in sealed cases. Put both in the same molecule ([[nitroglycerin]], [[tnt]], [[nitrocellulose]]) and you get explosives.

## In Ignis
- Oxidiser powders react with neighbouring fuel powders and liquids; alone they decompose when hot, releasing oxygen.
- Self-oxidising items ([[gunpowder]], [[fuse]], [[nitrocellulose]], explosives) need no air at all.

## Try this
Bury a pile of [[gunpowder]] under sand and light it with a hot wire (igniter). It burns anyway: no air needed.
