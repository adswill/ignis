# Inconel
*Nickel-chromium superalloy that keeps its strength while glowing red.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Density | 8190 kg/m³ (alloy 718) |
| Melting range | 1260-1336 °C (alloy 718) |
| Thermal conductivity | ≈11 W/(m·K) |
| Yield strength | ≈1030 MPa (718, aged) at room temperature, still ≈860 MPa at 650 °C |
| Oxidation resistance | to ≈980 °C (alloy 625) |

## In the real world
Superalloys are nickel-based alloys strengthened by tiny Ni₃(Al,Ti,Nb) precipitates that resist creep near their melting point. A chromium oxide skin protects them from hot oxygen. They are hard to machine, which is why many rocket parts are now 3D-printed in Inconel.

**Uses:** Gas-turbine discs and casings, exhaust manifolds, SpaceX Merlin and SuperDraco parts (printed Inconel), nuclear reactor parts.

**Hazards:** Expensive; nickel compounds are skin sensitisers.

## In Ignis
- Strength **5.0** and the highest softening temperature of the metals: **≈990 K**. Melts at **≈1620 K**.
- Low conductivity (0.35× steel): good for hot sections that must stay strong, not for spreading heat.
- The go-to material for jet-engine combustors and turbine sections.
