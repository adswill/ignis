# Ammonium perchlorate
*The oxidiser in almost every big solid rocket motor.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Formula | NH₄ClO₄ |
| Density | 1950 kg/m³ |
| Decomposition | starts ≈200 °C; deflagrates on its own above ≈20 atm |
| Share of Shuttle SRB propellant | 69.6 % |
| PEPCON plant explosion | Henderson, Nevada, 1988: ≈4000 t of AP |

## In the real world
AP is both a fuel (the ammonium) and an oxidiser (the perchlorate), so it can burn on its own under pressure. Mixed with aluminium and a rubbery binder it makes APCP, which flew the Space Shuttle and flies SLS. In 1988 a fire at the PEPCON plant in Nevada set off a series of explosions that registered as earthquakes.

**Uses:** Solid rocket motors, model rocket reloads, some fireworks.

**Hazards:** Strong oxidiser; large quantities can detonate in a fire. Perchlorate contamination in water interferes with the thyroid.

## In Ignis
- Decomposes above **≈570 K**, releasing oxygen.
- With a fuel powder: fast, hot reaction (**≈3000 K** flame) that speeds up with pressure.

## Recipes
- AP + [[aluminium_powder]] or [[sugar]] → [[apcp_mix]].

## Try this
Pack AP and aluminium powder into a steel tube with a narrow exit and light the top: a powder rocket motor.
