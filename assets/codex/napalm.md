# Napalm
*Gelled fuel that sticks to what it burns.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Napalm-B composition | ≈46 % polystyrene, 33 % gasoline, 21 % benzene |
| Burn temperature | ≈800-1200 °C |
| Invented | Harvard, 1942 (Louis Fieser's team) |

## In the real world
Thickening petrol into a gel makes it burn longer, stick to surfaces and resist being thrown off. The name comes from the original thickeners, naphthenic and palmitic acids. Its use on civilians is restricted by the 1980 Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons (Protocol III).

**Uses:** Military incendiary. Similar gelled fuels are used for wildfire backburning (drip torches, helitorches).

**Hazards:** Extremely difficult to extinguish; deep burns.

## In Ignis
- Viscous liquid: flows slowly and clings.
- Burns at its surface in air above **≈540 K** at **≈1290 K**, and evaporates fuel at **≈660 K**.

## Try this
Pour napalm and [[gasoline]] down a slope. Watch which one spreads and which one sticks.
