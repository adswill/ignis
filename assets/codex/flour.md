# Flour
*Harmless in a bag. Devastating as a cloud.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Bulk density | ≈500-600 kg/m³ |
| Heat of combustion | ≈15-17 MJ/kg |
| Minimum explosible concentration | ≈50-100 g/m³ of air |
| Washburn A Mill | Minneapolis, 1878: flour dust explosion, 18 dead |

## In the real world
A pile of flour hardly burns: air reaches only its surface. Suspend the same flour in air and every particle is surrounded by oxygen, so a flame races through the cloud and the heat makes the gas expand violently. Mills and grain silos have exploded this way for centuries.

**Uses:** Food. Also the textbook demonstration of dust explosions.

**Hazards:** Dust explosions; a first small blast can lift settled dust into a much bigger second explosion.

## In Ignis
- Burns at its surface in air above **≈660 K**; dust lifted into the air burns much faster than a heap.
- Leaves no ash.

## Try this
Put a [[fan]] under a heap of flour inside a box with an igniter. Turn the fan on and then the igniter.
