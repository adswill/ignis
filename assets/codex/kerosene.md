# Kerosene (RP-1)
*Jet fuel and the propellant of Saturn V's first stage.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Density | ≈800-820 kg/m³ |
| Boiling range | ≈150-300 °C |
| Flash point | ≥38 °C (Jet A) |
| Autoignition | ≈210-220 °C |
| Lower heating value | ≈43 MJ/kg |
| Flammability limits | ≈0.7-5 % vapour in air |
| Stoichiometric O/F with LOX | ≈3.4 (engines run ≈2.3-2.7, fuel-rich) |

## In the real world
Kerosene is a cut of crude oil with 10-16 carbon atoms per molecule. It is safe-ish to handle because it gives off little vapour at room temperature (flash point ≥38 °C), yet it holds a lot of energy per litre. RP-1 is a highly refined rocket grade; the Saturn V's five F-1 engines burned it with liquid oxygen, as does Falcon 9.

**Uses:** Jet fuel (Jet A, JP-8), rocket fuel (RP-1), lamps and heaters.

**Hazards:** Pool fires are very smoky (soot); vapour in a warm closed tank can explode.

## In Ignis
- Floats on [[water]]. Evaporates when warm and boils at **≈450 K**; the vapour is what burns.
- Pool fires are sooty: the flame is fuel-rich in its core.
- A fuel for [[ammonium_nitrate]] ([[anfo]]).

## Recipes
- Kerosene + [[ammonium_nitrate]] → [[anfo]].
- Kerosene + [[liquid_oxygen]] → violent reaction (rocket propellant pair).

## Try this
Pour kerosene on water and light it. Then add water to the burning pool from above: see what happens to the flame.
