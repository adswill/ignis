# Rust (iron oxide)
*Iron that has already burned, slowly. Thermite takes the oxygen back.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Formula | Fe₂O₃ (hematite) |
| Density | ≈5240 kg/m³ |
| Melting point | 1565 °C |
| Rust volume | ≈2-3× the iron it came from (why rust bursts concrete) |

## In the real world
Iron slowly reacts with oxygen and water to form hydrated iron oxides. Chemically rust is iron's 'ash': it can't burn any further. But aluminium binds oxygen even more strongly than iron, so a hot aluminium powder will rip the oxygen away from rust in the thermite reaction.

**Uses:** Pigment (red ochre), thermite, polishing compound (jeweller's rouge), catalyst in solid propellant (0.4 % in Shuttle SRBs).

**Hazards:** Harmless as a powder; the danger is what it can do with aluminium.

## In Ignis
- Inert by itself, slightly catalyses [[hydrogen_peroxide]] decomposition.
- Formed when [[iron_filings]] burn or rust in water.

## Recipes
- Rust + [[aluminium_powder]] → [[thermite]] (ignition ≈1350 K, makes molten iron).
- Rust + [[magnesium]] → magnesium thermite (easier to light).

## Try this
Put a mound of thermite on a steel floor over a cavity and light it with the [[torch]] for a long time. Watch molten iron burn through.
