# Molten glass
*Glowing, honey-thick liquid glass.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Working temperature | ≈1000-1200 °C (soda-lime) |
| Melting furnace | ≈1500 °C |
| Viscosity at working point | ≈10³ Pa·s (a million × water) |

## In the real world
Glass has no sharp melting point: it gradually softens as it heats. Glassblowers work it at around 1000 °C where it flows like thick honey, then anneal it (cool it slowly) so internal stresses don't crack it later.

**Uses:** Glassmaking, fibreglass, optical fibres.

**Hazards:** Radiant heat; molten glass splashes stick and burn.

## In Ignis
- Forms from [[sand]] at **≈1710 K** or from heated [[glass]]; starts at **≈1710 K**.
- Very viscous; freezes back into [[glass]] below **≈1350 K**.

## Try this
Hold the torch on a sand pile until it melts, then let it cool into glass.
