# Sulfur
*The low-temperature igniter of black powder; burns with a ghostly blue flame.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Density | 2070 kg/m³ (rhombic) |
| Melting point | 115.2 °C (388 K) |
| Boiling point | 444.6 °C (718 K) |
| Autoignition | ≈232 °C (505 K) |
| Heat of combustion | 9.3 MJ/kg (to SO₂) |

## In the real world
Sulfur burns to sulfur dioxide with a pale blue flame that is easy to miss in daylight. In black powder it lowers the ignition temperature and speeds up the burn. Most sulfur today comes from removing it from natural gas and oil.

**Uses:** Sulfuric acid (the most-produced industrial chemical), vulcanised rubber, fertiliser, fungicides, matches, black powder.

**Hazards:** SO₂ fumes are choking and toxic; sulfur dust is explosive.

## In Ignis
- Ignites in air at only **≈510 K** and burns at **≈1200 K**: a starter for other mixes.
- Helps saltpeter mixes catch fire.

## Recipes
- [[saltpeter]] + [[charcoal]] + sulfur (75:15:10) → [[black_powder]].

## Try this
Sprinkle a thin line of sulfur across a pile of [[charcoal]] and touch the end with the [[torch]]. The sulfur carries flame into the charcoal.
