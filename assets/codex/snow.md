# Snow
*Ice crystals full of trapped air: a fluffy insulator.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Density (fresh) | ≈50-200 kg/m³ |
| Melting point | 0 °C (273 K) |
| Thermal conductivity | ≈0.05-0.2 W/(m·K) fresh |
| Latent heat of fusion | 334 kJ/kg |

## In the real world
Snowflakes are ice crystals that grew from vapour in clouds. Fresh snow is 90-95 % air, which is why it insulates well enough for igloos and why it is so quiet after a snowfall. Melting it soaks up as much heat as warming the water from 0 to 80 °C.

**Uses:** Insulation, water storage in mountains, winter sports.

**Hazards:** Avalanches; snow loads collapse roofs.

## In Ignis
- Light powder at **≈260 K**; melts at **≈273 K** into [[water]], absorbing latent heat while it does.

## Try this
Drop snow onto a small fire. It melts, then the water boils: both soak up heat.
