# Wax
*Paraffin wax: a solid fuel that melts before it burns.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Density | ≈900 kg/m³ |
| Melting point | ≈46-68 °C |
| Flash point | ≈200-250 °C |
| Heat of combustion | ≈42-46 MJ/kg |
| A candle | burns ≈5-10 g of wax per hour, ≈80 W of heat |

## In the real world
Paraffin wax is a mix of long straight-chain hydrocarbons from petroleum. In 1861 Michael Faraday gave his famous lectures 'The Chemical History of a Candle', showing that the wick, melt pool, vapour and flame form a self-regulating little combustion engine.

**Uses:** Candles, coatings, crayons, investment casting, phase-change heat storage.

**Hazards:** A wax pan fire behaves like an oil fire.

## In Ignis
- Melts at **≈330 K** into [[molten_wax]], which boils into fuel vapour at **≈600 K**: the vapour burns.

## Try this
Build a candle: a wax column with a thin [[fuse]] or [[paper]] wick on top. Light the wick.
