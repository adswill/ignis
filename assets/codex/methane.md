# Methane
*CH₄: natural gas, the simplest hydrocarbon, and Ignis' main gas fuel.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Formula | CH₄ |
| Density | 0.657 kg/m³ at 25 °C (0.55× air) |
| Flammability limits | ≈5-15 % in air (newer data 4.4-16.4 %) |
| Autoignition | ≈537-600 °C |
| Lower heating value | 50.0 MJ/kg |
| Stoichiometric mix | 9.5 % in air; air/fuel mass ratio 17.2 |
| Adiabatic flame temperature | ≈2220 K in air, ≈3050 K in oxygen |
| Laminar flame speed | ≈0.37-0.40 m/s (stoichiometric, air) |
| Minimum ignition energy | ≈0.28 mJ |

## In the real world
CH₄ + 2 O₂ → CO₂ + 2 H₂O. Methane is the main component of natural gas, of biogas, of the 'firedamp' that explodes in coal mines, and of the atmospheres of Titan. It is lighter than air, so leaks rise. Its high hydrogen-to-carbon ratio gives the least CO₂ per unit energy of any hydrocarbon. LOX/methane is the propellant of SpaceX's Raptor and Blue Origin's BE-4.

**Uses:** Heating, cooking, power generation, hydrogen and ammonia production, rocket fuel.

**Hazards:** Explosive between ≈5 and 15 % in air; odourless (the smell is added mercaptan); a potent greenhouse gas.

## In Ignis
- The gas fuel species. Shares its slot with hydrogen, fuel vapours from liquids and pyrolysis gas from wood.
- Burns by finite-rate (Arrhenius) chemistry blended with turbulent mixing ([[eddy_dissipation_concept]]).
- Too lean or too rich and it won't burn: see [[flammability_limits]]. Without enough oxygen hot fuel cracks into [[soot]].
- Lighter than air: unburnt methane rises.

## Recipes
- Methane + [[air]] or [[oxygen]] → CO₂ + H₂O + heat ([[methane_combustion]]).

## Try this
Spray a short burst of fuel into still air and light it: a flame front. Now hold the spray much longer to make a rich cloud and light it: it only burns at the edges where it meets air.
