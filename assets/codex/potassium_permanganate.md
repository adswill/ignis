# Potassium permanganate
*Purple crystals that set glycerin on fire by themselves.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Formula | KMnO₄ |
| Density | 2700 kg/m³ |
| Decomposition | above ≈240 °C |
| With glycerin | ignites spontaneously after a delay of seconds to a minute |

## In the real world
Permanganate is a strong oxidiser that turns water vivid purple even at tiny concentrations. With glycerin the oxidation heats up slowly until it runs away into flame, with no spark needed. Firefighters drop permanganate capsules injected with glycol ('dragon eggs') from helicopters to light controlled burns.

**Uses:** Water treatment, disinfectant, aerial ignition for prescribed burns, chemistry teaching.

**Hazards:** Stains skin brown (manganese dioxide); strong oxidiser.

## In Ignis
- Decomposes above **≈510 K** releasing oxygen.
- With [[glycerin]] it heats up by itself and bursts into flame after a delay.
- A catalyst for [[hydrogen_peroxide]].

## Recipes
- KMnO₄ + [[glycerin]] → delayed spontaneous fire ([[permanganate_glycerin]]).

## Try this
Put a small heap of permanganate in a dish and pour glycerin on it. Don't touch the torch. Wait.
