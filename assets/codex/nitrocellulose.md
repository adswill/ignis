# Nitrocellulose (guncotton)
*Cotton soaked in nitric acid becomes a fuel with its own oxygen inside every molecule.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Ignition | ≈160-170 °C |
| Heat of explosion | ≈4 MJ/kg (13 % N) |
| Discovery | Christian Schönbein, 1846 |
| Residue | almost none: no smoke, no ash |

## In the real world
Nitrating cellulose attaches nitrate groups to every glucose unit, so each molecule carries fuel and oxidiser together. It flashes away in a puff of flame with no ash (flash paper). Gelatinised with solvents it became smokeless powder, which replaced black powder in guns.

**Uses:** Smokeless gunpowder, rocket propellant (double-base), lacquer and nail varnish, flash paper for magicians, early film (celluloid).

**Hazards:** Dry nitrocellulose degrades and can self-ignite; old nitrate film burns underwater.

## In Ignis
- Self-oxidising: ignites at **≈444 K** and burns extremely fast at **≈2700 K**, leaving no ash or smoke.
