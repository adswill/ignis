# Ash & slag
*What's left when everything that can burn has burned.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Wood ash content | ≈0.5-2 % of dry wood mass |
| Main components | calcium carbonate, potassium and magnesium salts |
| Bulk density | ≈500-900 kg/m³ |
| Thermite slag | mostly Al₂O₃ (melts 2072 °C) |

## In the real world
Ash is the non-combustible minerals left after burning. Wood ash soaked in water gives lye (potassium hydroxide), the original soap ingredient, and the word 'potassium' comes from 'pot ash'. Metal fires leave slag: molten oxides that freeze into glassy rock.

**Uses:** Soap making, fertiliser, glazes, cement additive (fly ash).

**Hazards:** Ash can hide live embers for days.

## In Ignis
- Inert residue from [[wood]], [[charcoal]], [[coal]], [[paper]], metal fires and thermite slag.
- Falls and piles like [[sand]].

## Try this
Burn a stack of paper and watch it shrink to a little grey pile of ash.
