# Magnesium
*Burns with a blinding white flame at around 3100 °C, and water makes it worse.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Density | 1738 kg/m³ |
| Melting / boiling point | 650 °C / 1091 °C |
| Heat of combustion | 24.7 MJ/kg |
| Flame temperature | ≈3100 °C |
| Burning in | air, CO₂, steam and even nitrogen |

## In the real world
Magnesium is a light structural metal, but as ribbon, chips or powder it burns with intense white light (used in early camera flash). It is so reactive that it pulls oxygen out of water, making hydrogen, and out of CO₂, so ordinary extinguishers feed the fire. Class D powder or dry sand is needed.

**Uses:** Light alloys (car wheels, laptop cases), flares and fireworks, thermite starters, sacrificial anodes.

**Hazards:** Do not look directly at burning magnesium (UV); do not use water on it.

## In Ignis
- Burns in air above **≈780 K** at **≈3090 K**, leaving white [[ash]].
- Hot magnesium reacts with [[water]], making hydrogen.
- Fizzes hard in [[hydrochloric_acid]].

## Recipes
- Magnesium + [[rust]] → magnesium [[thermite]] (lights easier).
- Magnesium + [[potassium_perchlorate]] → [[flash_powder]].

## Try this
Light magnesium powder, then spray [[water]] on it. Watch the hydrogen flare up.
