# Glycerin
*Sweet, syrupy, harmless, and the backbone of nitroglycerin.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Formula | C₃H₅(OH)₃ |
| Density | 1261 kg/m³ |
| Boiling point | 290 °C |
| Viscosity | ≈1.4 Pa·s at 20 °C (≈1400× water) |
| Flash / autoignition | ≈160 °C / ≈370 °C |

## In the real world
Glycerin is the backbone of every fat molecule, released when fats are made into soap or biodiesel. It is non-toxic and used in food and cosmetics. Nitrating it with nitric and sulfuric acids gives [[nitroglycerin]].

**Uses:** Food, cosmetics, antifreeze, e-cigarette liquid, nitroglycerin production.

**Hazards:** Low hazard by itself; ignites spontaneously with strong oxidisers like permanganate.

## In Ignis
- Very viscous: flows slowly. Boils at **≈570 K**.
- With [[potassium_permanganate]] it self-ignites after a delay.

## Recipes
- [[potassium_permanganate]] + glycerin → spontaneous fire ([[permanganate_glycerin]]).

## Try this
Pour glycerin and [[water]] from the same height and watch how differently they flow.
