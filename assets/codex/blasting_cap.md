# Blasting cap
*A tiny, very sensitive charge whose only job is to set off a big insensitive one.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Primary charge | lead azide or lead styphnate (historically mercury fulminate) |
| Base charge | PETN or RDX, ≈0.5-1 g |
| Invented | Alfred Nobel, 1865 (mercury fulminate cap) |

## In the real world
Safe explosives are designed to be hard to detonate. A detonator bridges the gap: a primary explosive that detonates from a spark or flame, whose shock then sets off a small base charge, whose shock is strong enough to start the main charge. This 'explosive train' is why commercial explosives can be handled safely.

**Uses:** Starting every commercial and military high explosive.

**Hazards:** Very sensitive to heat, shock, friction and static.

## In Ignis
- The most sensitive item: detonates above **≈600 K**, under a **≈4 atm** shock, or when touched by gas above **≈1050 K**.
- Its blast is enough to set off [[tnt]], [[c4]] and [[anfo]].

## Try this
Run a [[fuse]] to a blasting cap pressed against a big block of C-4. Light the fuse and stand well back.
