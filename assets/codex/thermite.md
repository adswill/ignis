# Thermite
*Fe₂O₃ + 2 Al → 2 Fe + Al₂O₃ + 852 kJ*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Energy | ≈4.0 MJ/kg of mixture |
| Adiabatic temperature | ≈3135 K (capped by iron boiling at 3134 K); ≈2500 °C typical in practice |
| Ignition | ≈1500 °C+: needs a magnesium ribbon or sparkler |
| Invented | Hans Goldschmidt, 1893-1898 |

## In the real world
Aluminium binds oxygen more tightly than iron, so a hot mixture of aluminium powder and iron oxide swaps the oxygen over, releasing enough heat to melt the iron produced. No gas is made, so it pours rather than explodes. It still welds railway track in the field, and burns through steel plate.

## In Ignis
- [[rust]] beside [[aluminium_powder]] ignites only above **≈1350 K** (keep the torch on it).
- Makes [[molten_metal]] at **≈2800 K** (freezes into steel) and slag ([[ash]]).
- With [[magnesium]] instead of aluminium it lights at **≈1050 K**.

## Try this
Put thermite on a steel plate over an empty box. Light it and watch the iron melt through the plate.
