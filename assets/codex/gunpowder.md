# Gunpowder
*Ready-mixed black powder: 75 % saltpeter, 15 % charcoal, 10 % sulfur.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Composition | 75 % KNO₃, 15 % charcoal, 10 % sulfur |
| Ignition | ≈430 °C |
| Heat of explosion | ≈2.7-3 MJ/kg |
| Gas produced | ≈0.27 m³/kg, plus ≈55 % solid residue (white smoke) |
| Combustion temperature | ≈2300 K |

## In the real world
Black powder deflagrates: its flame front moves much slower than sound, but the grains burn so quickly that a sealed container bursts. Over half its mass ends up as solid potassium salts, the white smoke of old battlefields. The modern recipe was fixed by the 1700s.

**Uses:** Fireworks lift charges, fuses, muzzle-loading guns, model rocket motors (Estes), historical mining.

**Hazards:** Sensitive to sparks, friction and static.

## In Ignis
- Self-oxidising mix: ignites at **≈690 K**, burns at **≈2200 K** with lots of white smoke.
- Loose, it whooshes; confined in a wall box, it bursts the box.

## Recipes
- Equivalent to mixing [[saltpeter]], [[charcoal]] and [[sulfur]] yourself: see [[black_powder]].

## Try this
Fill a small steel box with gunpowder and light it through a 1-cell hole. Then light the same amount in the open.
