# Gasoline (petrol)
*So volatile its vapour finds the flame before you do.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Density | ≈710-770 kg/m³ |
| Boiling range | ≈30-200 °C |
| Flash point | ≈-43 °C |
| Autoignition | ≈250-280 °C |
| Lower heating value | ≈43-44 MJ/kg |
| Flammability limits | ≈1.4-7.6 % vapour in air |

## In the real world
Gasoline is a blend of light hydrocarbons that evaporate readily even in winter (flash point about -43 °C). Its vapour is heavier than air and spreads along the ground. In engines it is compressed with air and lit by a spark; knocking happens when the mixture autoignites too early.

**Uses:** Car and small-engine fuel, solvent.

**Hazards:** Vapour explosions; never use it to start a fire. Contains benzene, a carcinogen.

## In Ignis
- The most volatile fuel: evaporates fast even at room temperature and boils at **≈360 K**.
- Its vapour cloud ignites from a distance: light the edge and the flame races back to the pool.

## Try this
Leave a gasoline puddle in a closed room for a while, then put an igniter in a far corner.
