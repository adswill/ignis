# Pyrolysis
*Breaking things apart with heat alone, no oxygen needed.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Wood pyrolysis | hemicellulose ≈200-300 °C, cellulose ≈300-400 °C, lignin ≈200-500 °C |
| Charcoal yield | ≈25-35 % of dry wood by mass (traditional kilns) |

Heat a solid fuel and its large molecules shake apart into smaller gases, tar vapours and a carbon-rich char. Pyrolysis is the first step of burning any solid: the gases it releases are what actually burn as flame. Done deliberately without air, it makes charcoal, coke and biochar. Plastics pyrolyse into waxy hydrocarbons, which is why they burn like oil.

## In Ignis
- [[wood]] pyrolyses above ≈600 K into fuel gas; hot fuel gas without oxygen cracks into [[soot]].

## Try this
Heat wood inside a sealed steel box with the torch from outside. Then open a vent: the fuel gas that built up ignites in the air.
