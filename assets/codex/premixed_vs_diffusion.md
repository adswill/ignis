# Premixed vs diffusion flames
*Mix first and burn, or burn where they meet.*

## Premixed flames
Fuel and air are mixed before they reach the flame, as in a Bunsen burner with its air hole open, a gas stove or a gasoline engine. The flame is a thin front that propagates into the mixture at the laminar flame speed. Clean, blue and hot.

## Diffusion (non-premixed) flames
Fuel and oxidiser arrive from different sides and burn where they meet, as in a candle, a campfire, a match or a diesel engine. The flame sits at the stoichiometric surface; the fuel-rich inside makes [[soot]], so it glows yellow.

## The Bunsen burner shows both
Close the air hole and you get a tall, lazy yellow diffusion flame. Open it and a crisp blue premixed cone appears, with a faint outer diffusion flame burning the leftovers.

## In Ignis
- Both kinds emerge on their own. A fuel jet into air makes a diffusion flame; a premixed cloud makes a travelling flame front.

## Try this
Build a Bunsen burner: a tube with a fuel inlet at the bottom. Try it with the side open to air and with it closed.
