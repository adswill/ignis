# Liquid oxygen (LOX)
*Pale blue, -183 °C, and willing to make almost anything burn violently.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Boiling point | 90.19 K (-183 °C) |
| Density | 1141 kg/m³ |
| Expansion to gas | ≈1:861 |
| Colour | pale blue (absorbs red light) |
| Magnetism | paramagnetic: sticks between magnet poles |

## In the real world
Liquid oxygen is the most common rocket oxidiser: it is dense, cheap and makes very hot flames. Soaked into anything porous and fuel-like (asphalt, clothing, charcoal) it can turn it into an explosive. In a rocket it is kept separate from fuel until the injector.

**Uses:** Rocket oxidiser (Saturn V, Falcon 9, Starship), medical and industrial oxygen storage, steelmaking.

**Hazards:** Cryogenic burns; oxygen-enriched atmospheres make clothes and hair burn fiercely.

## In Ignis
- Starts at **≈90 K** and boils quickly into pure oxygen gas: flames near it run much hotter.
- Any fuel touching it reacts violently (ignition ≈300 K, ≈3300 K flame).

## Recipes
- LOX + [[charcoal]] → [[oxyliquit]].
- LOX + [[kerosene]] → the classic rocket pair.

## Try this
Pour a little LOX next to a smouldering [[charcoal]] pile. Keep your distance.
