# Stone
*Rock: heavy, inert, and molten again above about 1200 °C.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Density | ≈2700-3000 kg/m³ |
| Melting range | ≈1200-1260 °C (basalt, granite) |
| Thermal conductivity | ≈1.5-3.5 W/(m·K) |
| Compressive strength | ≈100-250 MPa (granite) |

## In the real world
Rocks are mixtures of minerals. Igneous rocks like basalt and granite formed when lava or magma cooled; heat them back above about 1200 °C and they melt again. Stone is strong in compression but weak in tension and cracks when heated unevenly, which is how fire-setting was used in ancient mining.

**Uses:** Building, roads, kitchen counters, thermal mass.

**Hazards:** Rock heated by fire can spall (flake off explosively), especially if wet.

## In Ignis
- Static solid; melts into [[lava]] above **≈1230 K**.
- Lava that cools below **≈1080 K** becomes stone.
