# Molten wax
*Liquid paraffin: the fuel a candle wick actually burns.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Melting point | ≈46-68 °C (paraffin) |
| Flash point | ≈200-250 °C |
| Heat of combustion | ≈42-46 MJ/kg |

## In the real world
A candle flame melts a little pool of wax; the wick draws the liquid up by capillary action, where the flame's heat vaporises it. The vapour burns. Throw water on a pot of burning wax and you get a fireball, just like an oil fire.

**Uses:** Candles, investment casting, waterproofing.

**Hazards:** Wax fires behave like oil fires.

## In Ignis
- Freezes back into [[wax]] below **≈324 K**; boils into fuel vapour at **≈600 K**.
