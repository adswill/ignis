# Wood
*Wood doesn't burn: the gases it gives off when heated do.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Density | ≈400-750 kg/m³ (pine ≈500, oak ≈700) |
| Heat of combustion | ≈18-20 MJ/kg (dry) |
| Pyrolysis begins | ≈200-300 °C |
| Piloted ignition | ≈300-350 °C |
| Wood flame temperature | ≈1100-1300 K in the flame |
| Charring rate in fires | ≈0.6-0.8 mm/min (softwood, Eurocode 5) |

## In the real world
Heat breaks wood's cellulose, hemicellulose and lignin into a mix of flammable gases, tar droplets and char (pyrolysis). The visible flame is those gases burning in the air above the surface. The char layer insulates the wood underneath, which is why thick timber beams keep their strength in a fire far longer than you'd expect. Leftover minerals become [[ash]].

**Uses:** Fuel for about 2 billion people, charcoal making, construction.

**Hazards:** Smoke is rich in carbon monoxide and fine particles. Smouldering wood can reignite hours later.

## In Ignis
- Above **≈600 K** wood pyrolyses: it releases fuel gas that burns as flame in the air around it, and the solid slowly chars away.
- Very smoky when oxygen is short: the fuel gas cracks into [[soot]].
- Leaves [[ash]] behind; does not melt.
- The igniter heats touching wood, so a campfire is just logs plus a spark.

## Recipes
- Heat without air to make [[charcoal]] (real world).

Stack a small pile of logs in the open and light it. Then build the same pile inside a box with one small hole. Compare flame height and smoke in the **Fire** and **Gases** views.
