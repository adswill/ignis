# Cooking oil
*Vegetable oil: the reason you should never throw water on a pan fire.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Density | ≈910-930 kg/m³ |
| Smoke point | ≈160-230 °C |
| Flash point | ≈300-330 °C |
| Autoignition | ≈400-440 °C |
| Heat of combustion | ≈37 MJ/kg |

## In the real world
Oil is hard to ignite, but once a pan of oil reaches its autoignition temperature it bursts into flame. Water poured in sinks below the oil, flashes to steam and throws burning oil into the air as a fireball. The right response is to smother it with a lid or a fire blanket.

**Uses:** Cooking, biodiesel feedstock, lubricants.

**Hazards:** Grease fires; oily rags can self-heat and catch fire as they oxidise.

## In Ignis
- Boils at **≈600 K**; floats on [[water]].
- Water touching burning oil flashes to steam: [[steam_explosion]].

## Try this
Heat a pan of oil until it lights, then pour a little water onto it. Rebuild and smother it with a steel lid instead.

**See also:** [[steam_explosion]], [[flash_point]]
