# Combustion products (CO₂ + H₂O)
*What fire turns fuel and oxygen into.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| CO₂ density | 1.98 kg/m³ at 0 °C (1.5× air) |
| Water vapour density | ≈0.6 kg/m³ at 100 °C |
| Methane flame products | 1 CO₂ : 2 H₂O : 7.5 N₂ (by molecules, in air) |
| Atmospheric CO₂ | ≈420 ppm (2024) |

## In the real world
Complete combustion of hydrocarbons gives carbon dioxide and water. Both are strong infrared emitters and absorbers, which is why hot flame gases radiate heat and why CO₂ is a greenhouse gas. Incomplete combustion also gives carbon monoxide and soot.

**Uses:** CO₂ fire extinguishers, carbonation, dry ice, greenhouse enrichment; steam drives turbines.

**Hazards:** CO₂ accumulates in low spaces and suffocates; CO from incomplete combustion is deadly.

## In Ignis
- The products gas species: exhaust of every reaction, plus steam from boiling [[water]], CO₂ from [[dry_ice]], and acid fumes.
- Inert: a room full of products won't burn even if it is hot.

## Try this
Burn methane in a sealed box until the flame dies. Look in the **Gases** view: the oxygen is gone, replaced by products.
