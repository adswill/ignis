# Hydrogen
*The lightest gas and the most energy per kilogram of any chemical fuel.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Formula | H₂ |
| Density | 0.0899 kg/m³ at 0 °C (1/14 of air) |
| Flammability limits | 4-75 % in air |
| Autoignition | ≈500-585 °C |
| Lower heating value | 120 MJ/kg (2.4× methane) |
| Minimum ignition energy | ≈0.02 mJ (1/14 of methane) |
| Laminar flame speed | ≈2-3 m/s |
| Liquid hydrogen boiling point | 20.3 K |

## In the real world
2 H₂ + O₂ → 2 H₂O. Hydrogen ignites over a huge range of mixtures from the faintest spark, and its flame is nearly invisible in daylight. Burned with oxygen it gives the highest specific impulse of practical rocket propellants (≈450 s, RS-25). The Hindenburg (1937) was filled with it; the visible fire was largely the airship's skin and structure burning.

**Uses:** Ammonia and fertiliser production, refining, rocket fuel (Saturn V upper stages, Space Shuttle, SLS), fuel cells.

**Hazards:** Leaks easily, ignites easily, burns invisibly; embrittles steel.

## In Ignis
- Made by [[sodium]], [[potassium]], hot [[magnesium]] in water, and metals in [[hydrochloric_acid]] or [[lye]].
- In Ignis hydrogen shares the fuel-gas slot with methane, so it burns like methane does in the simulation.

## Recipes
- [[sodium]] + [[water]] → hydrogen ([[alkali_water]]).
- Metal + acid → hydrogen ([[acid_metal]]).

## Try this
Collect hydrogen from sodium in water under an upturned steel box, then put an igniter inside.
