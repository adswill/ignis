# Flash point
*The temperature at which a liquid gives off enough vapour to catch fire.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Gasoline | ≈-43 °C |
| Ethanol | 13 °C |
| Kerosene / Jet A | ≥38 °C |
| Diesel | ≥52 °C |
| Cooking oil | ≈300-330 °C |
| Fire point | usually a few to ≈10 °C above flash point: vapour sustains a flame |

Liquids don't burn: their **vapour** does. A liquid at its **flash point** makes just enough vapour to reach the lower [[flammability_limits]] above its surface, so a spark makes a brief flash. A little hotter (the **fire point**) and the flame keeps going. Regulations classify fuels by flash point: gasoline is far more dangerous to store than diesel even though they hold similar energy.

## In Ignis
- Volatile liquids ([[gasoline]], [[ethanol]], [[kerosene]] when warm) evaporate into fuel gas; heavier oils must be heated to near boiling before they burn.

## Try this
Light a pool of gasoline and a pool of diesel with the same short torch touch.
