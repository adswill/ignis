# Explosive (block)
*A generic TNT-class charge: turns to hot gas faster than it can move.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| TNT density | 1654 kg/m³ |
| Detonation velocity | ≈6900 m/s (TNT) |
| Heat of explosion | 4.184 MJ/kg (the definition of 'TNT equivalent') |
| Detonation pressure | ≈19-21 GPa |
| Products temperature | ≈3000-3500 K |

## In the real world
In a high explosive the reaction front is a shock wave moving faster than sound through the material itself. The solid becomes gas at nearly solid density, so pressure spikes to hundreds of thousands of atmospheres. The surrounding air is driven outward as a blast wave. Most military explosives are insensitive: they burn when lit and need a detonator to go off.

**Uses:** Mining, quarrying, demolition, avalanche control, metal forming, shaped charges.

**Hazards:** Blast overpressure: about 0.35 atm ruptures eardrums; above ~1 atm overpressure most buildings are destroyed.

## In Ignis
- Detonates above **≈600 K** or when hit by a shock above **≈8 atm**: the cell becomes **≈3450 K, 60 atm** gas instantly.
- Neighbouring charges are set off by the shock, so a line of explosive chain-reacts.
- Placeable matter explosives with different sensitivities: [[tnt]], [[c4]], [[dynamite]], [[nitroglycerin]], [[blasting_cap]].

## Try this
Place a charge in the middle of the empty scene and detonate it. Watch the **Pressure** view: a ring-shaped shock, then a slower expanding fireball.
