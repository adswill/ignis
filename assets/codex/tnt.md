# TNT
*Trinitrotoluene: the yardstick every other explosive is measured against.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Formula | C₇H₅N₃O₆ |
| Density | 1654 kg/m³ |
| Melting point | 80.4 °C |
| Detonation velocity | ≈6900 m/s |
| Heat of explosion | ≈4.6 MJ/kg (the 'TNT equivalent' is defined as 4.184 MJ/kg) |
| Detonation pressure | ≈19-21 GPa |

## In the real world
TNT melts at a safe 80 °C, so it can be poured into shells, and it is so insensitive that it burns rather than detonates when lit. It was first made as a yellow dye in 1863. It is oxygen-poor, so it detonates into a sooty black cloud and the excess carbon afterburns in air.

**Uses:** Military munitions, the energy unit for explosions and impacts (kilotons, megatons).

**Hazards:** Toxic (turned munitions workers' skin yellow); detonates reliably with a booster.

## In Ignis
- Burns slowly (ignites **≈570 K**) rather than detonating when lit.
- Detonates under a **≈25 atm** shock: needs a [[blasting_cap]] or another charge next to it.
- Its products are sooty.

## Try this
Light a TNT block with the torch: it just burns. Then put a [[blasting_cap]] against it and light the cap.
