# Steel
*Iron with a pinch of carbon: the workhorse of every pressure vessel.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Density | 7850 kg/m³ |
| Melting range | 1425-1540 °C (≈1700-1810 K) |
| Thermal conductivity | ≈45-50 W/(m·K) (plain carbon steel) |
| Specific heat | ≈490 J/(kg·K) |
| Yield strength | ≈250 MPa (structural A36) to >1500 MPa (tool steels) |
| Strength lost by 600 °C | about half |

## In the real world
Steel is iron alloyed with 0.05-2 % carbon. The carbon atoms lock iron's crystal planes so they cannot slide, turning soft iron into a tough, weldable, cheap structural metal. It stays strong to about 400 °C, then creeps and softens; by 600 °C a structural steel has roughly half its room-temperature strength, which is why steel buildings need fire protection.

**Uses:** Gas cylinders, boilers, pipelines, solid-rocket motor cases (the Space Shuttle boosters were steel tubes 3.7 m wide), bridges, cars.

**Hazards:** Fails by ductile bulging before bursting, which is a good thing: it gives warning. Hydrogen and cold can make it brittle.

## In Ignis
- The default wall. Strength rating **4.0** (aluminium 2, titanium 6.5).
- Starts to lose strength above **≈510 K** and melts at **≈1500 K** into flowing [[molten_metal]] that refreezes as steel.
- Moderate conductor: a hot spot spreads slowly, so a flame licking one spot can burn through.

## Recipes
- [[thermite]] makes molten iron that freezes into steel walls.
