# Ice
*Frozen water: floats because it is less dense than the liquid.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Density | 917 kg/m³ (water: 1000) |
| Melting point | 0 °C (273.15 K) |
| Latent heat of fusion | 334 kJ/kg |
| Thermal conductivity | ≈2.2 W/(m·K) |
| Above water when floating | ≈1/10 of its volume |

## In the real world
Water molecules lock into an open hexagonal lattice when they freeze, which takes up about 9 % more space than the liquid. That is why ice floats, why pipes burst in winter and why lakes freeze from the top down, protecting the life underneath.

**Uses:** Cooling, food preservation, ice rinks, ice cores as climate records.

**Hazards:** Expansion on freezing cracks rock and pipes.

## In Ignis
- Starts at **≈255 K**; melts at **≈273 K** into [[water]], holding at the melting point while it absorbs latent heat.
- Melts at **≈252 K** next to [[salt]].

## Recipes
- Ice + [[salt]] → [[brine]] (melts below 0 °C).

## Try this
Build an ice wall and fire a torch at it. It soaks up a surprising amount of heat before it's gone.
