# Aluminium powder
*A metal that burns at 3000 K once you get it going.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Density (metal) | 2700 kg/m³ |
| Heat of combustion | 31 MJ/kg (to Al₂O₃) |
| Melting / boiling point | 660 °C / 2470 °C |
| Oxide melting point | 2072 °C (the oxide skin delays ignition) |
| Flame temperature | ≈3000-3500 K |

## In the real world
Aluminium releases more heat per kilogram than wood and nearly as much per litre as any fuel, but its oxide skin stops it burning until it is very hot or very finely divided. Rocket engineers add 16-18 % aluminium to solid propellants because it raises flame temperature and density; the white smoke from boosters is aluminium oxide.

**Uses:** Solid rocket propellant, thermite welding, fireworks (white sparks and flash), silver paint.

**Hazards:** Fine aluminium powder is a severe dust-explosion hazard. Never use water on burning aluminium: it makes hydrogen.

## In Ignis
- Burns in air above **≈930 K** at **≈3000 K**, leaving [[ash]] (slag).
- Dissolves in [[lye]] and [[hydrochloric_acid]], making hydrogen.

## Recipes
- [[rust]] + aluminium powder → [[thermite]].
- [[copper_oxide]] + aluminium powder → [[copper_thermite]].
- [[ammonium_perchlorate]] + aluminium powder → [[apcp_mix]].
- [[potassium_perchlorate]] + aluminium powder → [[flash_powder]].

## Try this
Put aluminium powder next to [[rust]] on a steel plate and heat it with the [[torch]]. Thermite needs patience: it ignites only at very high temperature.
