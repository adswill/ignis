# Molten plastic
*Melted polyethylene: drips, flows, and feeds a fire as it goes.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Polyethylene melting | ≈105-135 °C |
| Pyrolysis | above ≈400 °C into waxy hydrocarbon vapours |
| Heat of combustion | ≈46 MJ/kg |

## In the real world
Burning plastic melts and drips, carrying the fire downward. The molten plastic breaks down into hydrocarbon vapours, which is why plastic fires are so smoky and so energetic: polyethylene holds as much energy per kilogram as fuel oil.

**Uses:** Injection moulding, extrusion, 3D printing.

**Hazards:** Burning drips spread fire; smoke is toxic.

## In Ignis
- Freezes back into [[plastic]] below **≈420 K**; boils into fuel vapour at **≈690 K**, which burns very smokily.
