# Flame colour
*Blue from molecules, yellow from soot, and every colour from metals.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Wien's law | λ_max = 2898 µm·K / T |
| Dull red glow | ≈800-900 K (Draper point ≈798 K) |
| Cherry red | ≈1100 K |
| Yellow-white | ≈1500-1800 K |
| Sodium | yellow, 589 nm |
| Potassium | lilac |
| Copper | blue-green |
| Strontium / lithium | red |
| Barium | green |

## Two sources of light
- **Chemiluminescence**: excited molecules in the reaction zone (CH*, C₂*, OH*) emit specific colours: the blue of a gas stove.
- **Incandescence**: hot solid particles ([[soot]], sparks, embers) glow with a black-body spectrum that shifts from red to white as they get hotter.

## Metals colour flames
Heat excites electrons in metal atoms, which emit sharp spectral lines as they fall back. Fireworks use strontium for red, barium for green, copper for blue and sodium for yellow.

## In Ignis
- The renderer colours hot gas and solids by temperature, like a black body, and lets soot glow.

## Try this
Watch a solid heated with the torch: dull red, cherry, orange, yellow, white as it heats.
