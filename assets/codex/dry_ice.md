# Dry ice
*Frozen CO₂ at -78.5 °C that turns straight into gas.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Sublimation point | -78.5 °C (194.7 K) at 1 atm |
| Density | ≈1400-1600 kg/m³ |
| Latent heat of sublimation | 571 kJ/kg |
| Gas expansion | ≈845× by volume |
| CO₂ gas density | 1.98 kg/m³ (1.6× air) |

## In the real world
At atmospheric pressure CO₂ cannot be liquid, so the solid sublimes directly into gas. The cold gas chills moisture into fog that rolls along the floor because CO₂ is heavier than air.

**Uses:** Shipping frozen goods and vaccines, stage fog, blast cleaning, carbonating drinks.

**Hazards:** CO₂ collects in low places and can suffocate; dry ice in a sealed bottle bursts it.

## In Ignis
- Starts at **≈195 K** and sublimes into cold CO₂ gas (product slot): it smothers flames.
- Heavy cold gas sinks and spreads along the floor.

## Try this
Put dry ice in a closed steel box and watch the pressure climb in the **Pressure** view until the box bursts.
