# Charcoal
*Wood cooked without air until only carbon is left.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Carbon content | ≈85-98 % |
| Heat of combustion | ≈29-33 MJ/kg |
| Bulk density | ≈200-600 kg/m³ |
| Ignition temperature | ≈350-400 °C |
| Glowing combustion | ≈1100-1300 °C with forced air |

## In the real world
Heating wood without enough oxygen drives off water, tars and gases (pyrolysis), leaving a light porous carbon skeleton. Charcoal burns as a glow rather than a tall flame because there is little volatile gas left: oxygen attacks the hot carbon surface directly, making CO and CO₂. For 3000 years it was the only fuel hot enough to smelt iron.

**Uses:** Barbecues, blacksmithing, black powder (its fine porous structure makes it react fast), water filters (activated carbon), art.

**Hazards:** Burning charcoal makes large amounts of carbon monoxide; never use it indoors.

## In Ignis
- Burns at its surface in air above **≈690 K**, glowing at **≈1500 K**, and needs oxygen from the surrounding gas.
- Fine charcoal in the air (a dust cloud) burns much faster than a heap.
- A key fuel for oxidiser mixes.

## Recipes
- [[saltpeter]] + charcoal (+ [[sulfur]]) → [[black_powder]].
- [[ammonium_nitrate]] + charcoal → [[anfo]]-like mix.
- [[liquid_oxygen]] + charcoal → [[oxyliquit]].
