# Molten metal
*2800 K liquid iron: flows, sets things alight, and freezes into steel.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Iron melting point | 1538 °C (1811 K) |
| Iron boiling point | 2862 °C |
| Liquid iron density | ≈6980 kg/m³ |
| Blast furnace tapping temperature | ≈1500 °C |

## In the real world
Molten iron glows yellow-white and radiates intense heat. Foundries pour it into sand moulds, and thermite welders pour it into a mould around two rail ends to join them. It is heavier than almost everything it lands on, so it sinks through other materials.

**Uses:** Casting, steelmaking, thermite rail welding.

**Hazards:** Water trapped under molten metal turns to steam instantly: foundry steam explosions are a major cause of injuries.

## In Ignis
- Starts at **≈2800 K** (thermite product) or forms when a metal wall melts; remembers which metal it was.
- Sets fire to burnables it touches and melts lower-melting walls.
- Freezes back into a wall of its original metal as it cools.
- With [[water]] it causes a [[steam_explosion]].

## Recipes
- [[thermite]] → molten iron.
- Molten metal + [[sand]] → [[molten_glass]].

## Try this
Pour molten metal onto an [[ice]] block on one side and a [[wood]] plank on the other.
