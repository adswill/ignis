# Nitrogen
*78 % of the air: the passenger in every flame.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Formula | N₂ |
| Density | 1.251 kg/m³ at 0 °C |
| Fraction of air | 78.08 % by volume |
| Bond | triple bond, 945 kJ/mol: very hard to break |
| Thermal NOx | forms above ≈1800 K (Zeldovich mechanism) |

## In the real world
Nitrogen's triple bond makes it almost inert, so in combustion it is mostly a heat sink that dilutes and cools the flame. At very high flame temperatures some nitrogen does react, forming nitrogen oxides (NOx), a key air pollutant. Explosives are full of nitrogen because turning nitrogen compounds into N₂ releases so much energy.

**Uses:** Inerting fuel tanks and food packaging, fertiliser (via ammonia), tyre inflation, cryogenics.

**Hazards:** An odourless asphyxiant: you can't feel it replacing your oxygen.

## In Ignis
- The inert gas species: dilutes, carries heat, doesn't burn.
- Comes from [[air]] and boiling [[liquid_nitrogen]].

## Try this
Flood a burning box with liquid nitrogen. The fire goes out from cooling and dilution.
