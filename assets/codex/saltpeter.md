# Saltpeter (KNO₃)
*Potassium nitrate: the oxidiser that made gunpowder possible.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Formula | KNO₃ |
| Density | 2109 kg/m³ |
| Melting point | 334 °C (607 K) |
| Decomposition | above ≈400 °C: 2 KNO₃ → 2 KNO₂ + O₂ |
| Oxygen available | ≈40 % of its mass is oxygen |

## In the real world
Saltpeter was once scraped from cave walls and manure heaps, where bacteria make nitrates. It stores oxygen in a solid that can be mixed intimately with fuel, so the fuel can burn far faster than air could ever reach it. Mixing it into charcoal and sulfur in 9th-century China gave the world black powder.

**Uses:** Fertiliser, food preservation (curing meat), black powder, fireworks, rocket candy, smoke bombs, stump remover.

**Hazards:** An oxidiser: mixtures with almost any fuel burn fiercely.

## In Ignis
- Decomposes above **≈675 K**, releasing oxygen into the gas around it: it feeds nearby fires.
- Reacts with neighbouring fuel powders (ignition **≈600 K**, flame **≈2200 K**).

## Recipes
- Saltpeter + [[charcoal]] (+ [[sulfur]]) → [[black_powder]].
- Saltpeter + [[sugar]] → [[rocket_candy]].
- Saltpeter + [[iron_filings]] → [[sparklers]].

## Try this
Put a saltpeter pile next to a smouldering [[charcoal]] heap and watch the flame jump as oxygen is released.
