# Fuse
*A slow-burning cord that gives you time to walk away.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Invented | William Bickford, Cornwall, 1831 |
| Core | black powder wrapped in textile and waterproofing |
| Standard burn rate | ≈120 s per metre (≈0.8 cm/s), ±10 % |

## In the real world
Before the safety fuse, miners lit charges with straws or goose quills of gunpowder, which burned unpredictably and killed many. Bickford's fuse wraps a thin black-powder core in tightly wound jute so it burns at a steady, known rate.

**Uses:** Mining, fireworks (visco fuse), demolition timing.

**Hazards:** Damaged fuse can burn faster or 'side-spit'.

## In Ignis
- Self-oxidising: ignites at **≈540 K** and burns slowly along its length at **≈1200 K**.
- Draw a line of fuse to a charge to make a delay.
