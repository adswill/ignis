# Salt (NaCl)
*Table salt: dissolves in water and melts ice down to -21 °C.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Formula | NaCl |
| Density | 2165 kg/m³ |
| Melting point | 801 °C |
| Solubility | ≈360 g per litre water at 25 °C |
| Eutectic with water | -21.1 °C (252 K) at 23.3 % salt |

## In the real world
Salt dissolves into sodium and chloride ions, which get in the way of water molecules trying to form ice crystals; that lowers the freezing point (freezing-point depression). That is why road salt melts ice down to about -21 °C. Salt water also conducts electricity and speeds up the rusting of iron.

**Uses:** Food, de-icing roads, chlorine and sodium hydroxide production (chlor-alkali process), preserving.

**Hazards:** Corrodes steel (salt water rusts iron several times faster).

## In Ignis
- Dissolves in [[water]] to make [[brine]].
- Next to [[ice]] it lowers the melting point to **≈252 K**.
- Boiling brine dry leaves salt crystals behind.

## Recipes
- Salt + [[water]] → [[brine]].
- [[lye]] + [[hydrochloric_acid]] → salt water ([[neutralisation]]).

## Try this
Build an ice block and sprinkle salt on one side. That side melts first even though nothing is heating it.
