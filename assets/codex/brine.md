# Brine (salt water)
*Salt dissolved in water: denser, harder to freeze, and hard on steel.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Seawater salinity / density | ≈3.5 %, ≈1025 kg/m³ |
| Saturated brine density | ≈1200 kg/m³ (26 % NaCl) |
| Boiling point (saturated) | ≈108.7 °C |
| Freezing point | down to -21.1 °C (eutectic) |

## In the real world
Dissolved ions make brine denser than water, raise its boiling point and lower its freezing point. Evaporating brine is how sea salt is made. Salt water speeds up corrosion because it conducts the tiny electric currents rusting needs.

**Uses:** Food preservation, de-icing, refrigeration coolant, salt production.

**Hazards:** Accelerates corrosion of iron and steel.

## In Ignis
- Boils at **≈375 K**; when it boils down it leaves [[salt]] behind.
- Freezes at **≈252 K**. Sinks under fresh water. Rusts iron faster than plain water.

## Recipes
- [[salt]] + [[water]] → brine.
- [[lye]] + [[hydrochloric_acid]] → brine + heat.

## Try this
Heat a pan of brine until it boils dry and look at what is left.
