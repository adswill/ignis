# Leidenfrost effect
*On a hot enough surface, a drop floats on its own steam.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Water on a metal plate | Leidenfrost point ≈190-200 °C |
| Described | Johann Gottlob Leidenfrost, 1756 |

Splash water on a pan at about 150 °C and it sizzles away instantly. On a pan above about 200 °C the drops skitter around and last much longer: the underside boils into a thin vapour layer that insulates the drop from the metal. Liquid nitrogen does the same on anything at room temperature. In boilers and quenching metal, the vapour film (film boiling) slows heat transfer dramatically, which can be dangerous.

## In Ignis
- Cryogenic liquids ([[liquid_nitrogen]], [[liquid_oxygen]]) boil furiously on warm surfaces.

## Try this
Pour liquid nitrogen across a warm floor and watch it boil off.
