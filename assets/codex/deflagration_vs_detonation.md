# Deflagration vs detonation
*A flame that spreads by heating its neighbours vs a shock wave that drags a flame behind it.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Laminar flame speed, methane-air | ≈0.4 m/s |
| Laminar flame speed, hydrogen-air | ≈2-3 m/s |
| Turbulent deflagration | tens to hundreds of m/s |
| Detonation, methane-air | ≈1800 m/s (Chapman-Jouguet) |
| Detonation, hydrogen-air | ≈1970 m/s |
| Detonation, TNT / RDX | ≈6900 / ≈8750 m/s |

## Deflagration: subsonic
In a **deflagration** the reaction zone advances because heat and radicals diffuse into the fresh mixture ahead of it. It moves slower than sound, so pressure has time to even out. In the open it makes a *whoosh*; in a container the gas expansion builds pressure until the container bursts (black powder, a gas explosion in a house).

## Detonation: supersonic
In a **detonation** a shock wave compresses and heats the mixture so violently that it reacts immediately behind the shock, and the energy released keeps the shock going. It moves faster than sound in the unreacted material, with a pressure jump of ~15-20× in gases and hundreds of thousands of atmospheres in solid explosives. Its speed is set by thermodynamics (the Chapman-Jouguet velocity), not by how fast heat diffuses.

## Deflagration-to-detonation transition (DDT)
A deflagration in a long pipe full of obstacles accelerates: turbulence wrinkles the flame, it burns faster, pushes the gas ahead faster, makes more turbulence... until a shock forms strong enough to become a detonation. Mines and gas pipelines are designed to stop this.

## In Ignis
- Gas flames, wood, black powder and propellants **deflagrate**.
- [[explosive]] blocks, [[tnt]], [[c4]], [[dynamite]], [[nitroglycerin]], [[blasting_cap]] and [[anfo]] **detonate**: the cell becomes very hot, very dense gas instantly.
- Insensitive explosives need a strong shock: build an explosive train.
