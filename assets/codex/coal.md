# Coal
*Ancient swamp plants, cooked by pressure and time into a carbon-rich rock.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Heat of combustion | ≈24-35 MJ/kg (lignite to anthracite) |
| Density | ≈1200-1500 kg/m³ (solid) |
| Ignition temperature | ≈400-450 °C |
| Courrières mine disaster | France, 1906: coal-dust explosion, 1099 dead |

## In the real world
Coal formed from plants buried 300 million years ago. It contains carbon plus volatile hydrocarbons, so unlike charcoal it burns with a smoky flame at first. Coal powered the industrial revolution and still makes about a third of the world's electricity.

**Uses:** Power stations, steelmaking (as coke), heating.

**Hazards:** Coal dust explodes; mines also fill with methane ('firedamp'). Burning coal is the largest single source of CO₂.

## In Ignis
- Burns in air above **≈720 K** at **≈1650 K**, leaving [[ash]].
- Burns slowly as a heap, explosively as a dust cloud.

## Try this
Fill a tunnel with [[methane]], then add coal dust and light one end. The methane flame lifts the dust and the explosion grows. See [[dust_explosions]].
