# Iron filings
*Fine iron that throws bright sparks when it burns.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Density (metal) | 7874 kg/m³ |
| Melting point | 1538 °C (1811 K) |
| Heat of combustion | ≈7.4 MJ/kg (to Fe₂O₃) |
| Hand warmers | iron powder + salt water + air: ≈60 °C for hours |

## In the real world
Solid iron does not burn in air, but fine iron particles do, as the bright branching sparks of a grinder or sparkler. In water and air iron slowly rusts; disposable hand warmers just speed that reaction up with salt and a catalyst.

**Uses:** Sparklers, hand warmers, magnetism demonstrations, oxygen absorbers in food packets.

**Hazards:** Fine iron dust can be pyrophoric (ignites by itself in air) when freshly made.

## In Ignis
- Burns in air above **≈840 K** at **≈1800 K** and turns into [[rust]].
- Rusts slowly when wet, faster in [[brine]]. Fizzes slowly in [[hydrochloric_acid]].
- Mixed with oxidisers it burns slower but sparkier.

## Recipes
- [[saltpeter]] + iron filings → [[sparklers]].
- Iron filings + water → [[rust]] ([[rusting]]).

## Try this
Leave iron filings in [[brine]] next to some in fresh [[water]] and run the simulation fast. Which rusts first?
