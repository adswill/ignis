# Oxygen
*The 21 % of air that does all the work in a fire.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Formula | O₂ |
| Density | 1.429 kg/m³ at 0 °C (1.1× air) |
| Fraction of air | 20.95 % by volume, 23.1 % by mass |
| Boiling point | 90.2 K |
| Methane flame | ≈2220 K in air → ≈3050 K in pure O₂ |

## In the real world
In air, every molecule of oxygen comes with nearly four molecules of nitrogen that soak up flame heat without contributing. Remove the nitrogen and flames get far hotter and faster, which is how oxy-fuel torches cut steel. Oxygen-enriched atmospheres are a severe fire hazard: the Apollo 1 fire (1967) raced through a cabin of 100 % oxygen at 1.1 atm.

**Uses:** Rocket oxidiser, steelmaking (basic oxygen furnace), cutting and welding, medicine.

**Hazards:** Makes ordinary materials burn violently; never oil an oxygen fitting.

## In Ignis
- The oxidiser gas species. Comes from [[air]], [[oxygen_inlet]]s, boiling [[liquid_oxygen]] and decomposing oxidisers.
- More oxygen → hotter flame (less nitrogen to heat).
