# Liquid nitrogen
*77 K, completely inert, and it boils on contact with everything.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Boiling point | 77.36 K (-196 °C) |
| Density | 808 kg/m³ |
| Expansion to gas | ≈1:694 |
| Latent heat | 199 kJ/kg |

## In the real world
Nitrogen gas makes up 78 % of air, and liquefying it is cheap. Dropped on a warm surface it floats on a cushion of its own vapour and skitters about (the [[leidenfrost_effect]]). It is inert, so it does not feed fires, but the gas it makes can displace oxygen in a closed room.

**Uses:** Cryopreservation, freezing food, cooling superconductors and infrared sensors, liquid-nitrogen ice cream.

**Hazards:** Asphyxiation in enclosed spaces; sealed containers burst from the pressure.

## In Ignis
- Starts at **≈77 K** and boils into inert nitrogen gas.
- Cools anything it touches; the cold gas sinks.

## Try this
Pour liquid nitrogen into a sealed steel box and watch the pressure climb. Then pour it over a fire.
