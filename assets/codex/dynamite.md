# Dynamite
*Nitroglycerin made safe(r) by soaking it into a powder.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Original recipe | ≈75 % nitroglycerin, 25 % diatomaceous earth (Nobel, 1867) |
| Density | ≈1300-1500 kg/m³ |
| Detonation velocity | ≈4000-7000 m/s depending on grade |

## In the real world
Alfred Nobel found that absorbing nitroglycerin into kieselguhr (diatomaceous earth) made it stable enough to transport and handle, while a blasting cap would still set it off. Dynamite built tunnels, canals and railways worldwide and funded the Nobel Prizes.

**Uses:** Mining, quarrying, tunnelling (largely replaced by ANFO and emulsions today).

**Hazards:** Old dynamite 'sweats' nitroglycerin, which is extremely sensitive.

## In Ignis
- Much less sensitive than [[nitroglycerin]]: detonates above **≈780 K** or under a **≈7 atm** shock. Burns when lit (ignites **≈570 K**).

## Recipes
- Real world: [[nitroglycerin]] + diatomaceous earth.

## Try this
Drill a 'tunnel' through [[stone]] with a row of dynamite sticks and a blasting cap at one end.
