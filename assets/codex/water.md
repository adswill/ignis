# Water
*The best fire extinguisher there is, because of how much heat it takes to boil it.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Density | 997 kg/m³ at 25 °C (max 999.97 at 4 °C) |
| Boiling point | 100 °C (373.15 K) at 1 atm |
| Specific heat | 4.18 kJ/(kg·K) |
| Latent heat of vaporisation | 2257 kJ/kg |
| Latent heat of fusion | 334 kJ/kg |
| Liquid → steam expansion | ≈1600-1700× at 1 atm, 100 °C |

## In the real world
Water has an unusually high heat capacity and an enormous heat of vaporisation thanks to hydrogen bonds. Boiling a litre of water absorbs 2.26 MJ, about five times what it took to heat it from 0 to 100 °C. Firefighters use it to cool fuel below its ignition point, and the steam pushes away oxygen. But water poured onto very hot things flashes to steam with explosive force.

**Uses:** Fire suppression, coolant in power stations and engines, steam turbines, everything alive.

**Hazards:** Never use water on burning oil, metal fires, or molten metal: see [[steam_explosion]].

## In Ignis
- Boils at **≈373 K**, absorbing latent heat; the steam goes into the gas as combustion products (inert).
- Freezes into [[ice]] at **≈273 K**.
- Touching [[molten_metal]], [[lava]] or burning oil it flashes violently to steam.
- Dissolves [[salt]] into [[brine]]; reacts with [[sodium]], [[potassium]] and hot [[magnesium]]; rusts [[iron_filings]].

## Recipes
- Water + [[salt]] → [[brine]].
- Water + [[sodium]] → [[lye]] + hydrogen.
- Water + [[molten_metal]] → [[steam_explosion]].

## Try this
Light a [[wood]] campfire, then pour a stream of water over it. Watch the temperature drop in the **Heat** view as the water boils.
