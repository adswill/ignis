# Paper
*Thin sheets of cellulose: catches fire around 230 °C.*

## At a glance
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Density | ≈700-1200 kg/m³ |
| Autoignition | ≈218-246 °C (Fahrenheit 451 = 233 °C) |
| Heat of combustion | ≈15-17 MJ/kg |
| Invented | China, ≈105 AD (Cai Lun) |

## In the real world
Paper is a mat of cellulose fibres. Its thinness gives a huge surface for its mass, so it heats up and ignites quickly; that is why kindling and paper start fires that big logs can't. Ray Bradbury's 'Fahrenheit 451' took its title from paper's approximate autoignition temperature.

**Uses:** Writing, packaging, kindling.

**Hazards:** Burning paper lifts burning fragments in the plume, spreading fire.

## In Ignis
- Burns at its surface in air above **≈507 K** at **≈1080 K**, leaving [[ash]].
- Great kindling for [[wood]].

## Try this
Build a wood log fire with and without a few paper cells underneath. Which lights first with the same torch time?
